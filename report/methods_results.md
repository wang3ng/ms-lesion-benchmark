## 2 Methods

### 2.1 Data

We used the preprocessed release of the MSSEG-2016 challenge [REF], in which images were denoised, registered, brain-extracted and bias-corrected by the organisers <!-- src: README.md -->. The reference for every MSSEG case is the challenge consensus of seven expert delineations [REF] <!-- src: README.md -->. The 15 training patients come from three scanners, 5 each from a Siemens Verio 3T, a Siemens Aera 1.5T and a Philips Ingenia 3T <!-- src: README.md -->. The 38 test scans comprise 10 per training scanner and 8 from a GE Discovery 3T that is absent from training and serves as the unseen-scanner test <!-- src: README.md -->. As an external test set we used the 21 labelled scans of the ISBI 2015 longitudinal challenge [REF], which come from 5 patients scanned on one Philips 3T system <!-- src: results/test/summary.md -->. ISBI provides two raters and no consensus, so we scored against each rater separately. Table 1 summarises the data. Only FLAIR, T1 and T2 were used, as these are the contrasts shared by all datasets; all images and masks were kept on the FLAIR grid of each case <!-- src: data/README.md -->.

Model development used patient-level 5-fold cross-validation (CV) on the 15 MSSEG training patients, with one patient per scanner in each validation fold and seed 20261011 <!-- src: results/E1/cv_summary.md -->. All design choices (training length, lesion-size bins, the E8 decision and the post-hoc corrections described below) were made on the CV folds; no test case was used for any choice. The exploratory experiment E9 additionally used 84 scans of 49 patients from the MSLesSeg training release [REF] (Philips Ingenia and Philips Achieva dStream), after excluding 9 scans from GE or unknown scanners so that the GE test set stayed unseen <!-- src: results/E9/training_set.json -->.

### 2.2 Models and conditions

**E1, nnU-Net.** We trained nnU-Net v2 [REF] in its `3d_fullres` configuration with the default plans (PlainConvUNet with six stages, patch 96 × 160 × 160, batch size 2, target spacing 1.1 × 0.744 × 0.744 mm) and the default trainer for 1000 epochs in every fold <!-- src: experiments/E1/README.md -->. Each fold took about 9.2 h <!-- src: results/E1/cv_summary.md -->. Test predictions are the 5-fold ensemble <!-- src: experiments/test_stage/README.md -->.

**MedSAM conditions (E2a, E2b, E2c, E8).** All MedSAM conditions use MedSAM ViT-B [REF] with a frozen image encoder. Axial slices containing brain were embedded once and cached (74 volumes, 14,180 slices) <!-- src: experiments/E2/README.md -->. FLAIR, T1 and T2 formed the three input channels, each clipped to its 0.5–99.5 brain percentile and scaled to [0, 1], on a 1024 × 1024 canvas that preserves the physical aspect ratio <!-- src: experiments/E2/README.md -->. For each condition and fold we fine-tuned the prompt encoder and mask decoder for 10,000 steps of 16 boxes (AdamW, learning rate 1e-4, weight decay 0.01, 200 warm-up steps, bf16), keeping the last checkpoint <!-- src: experiments/E2/README.md -->. The training target is the reference restricted to the box. At inference, each pixel receives the mean sigmoid probability over the boxes covering it, thresholded at 0.5, and slices are stacked to 3D <!-- src: experiments/E2/README.md -->. Each condition uses the same box type at training and test time. **E2a** uses oracle boxes, one per 2D 8-connected reference component per slice with each side enlarged by 0–5 voxels; because the boxes come from the reference, E2a is an upper bound on tracing rather than a fair comparison <!-- src: experiments/E2/README.md -->. **E2b** uses the same box construction around E1's predictions: out-of-fold E1 masks for training patients and the E1 test ensemble for test scans <!-- src: experiments/E2b/PLAN.md -->. **E2c**, the fair head-to-head with E1, uses one whole-brain box per slice plus a 4 × 4 grid of tiles with 25% overlap inside the brain bounding box, including lesion-free tiles; training batches contain 50% boxes with lesion voxels <!-- src: experiments/E2/README.md -->. **E8** is one targeted fix, chosen by a rule fixed in the configuration before any MedSAM training: if the pooled recall of lesions < 20 mm³ in CV satisfied recall(E2a) − recall(E2c) ≥ 0.15, the fix would be zoomed tiles; otherwise, if recall(E2a) < 0.6, a small-lesion-weighted loss; otherwise zoomed tiles <!-- src: configs/experiment.yaml -->. The rule gave 0.6692 − 0.1504 = 0.5188 and therefore selected zoomed tiles <!-- src: results/E2/tables/e8_decision.json -->. E8 is E2c with each tile cropped and re-embedded on its own canvas (about 4× zoom), with the whole-brain box unchanged <!-- src: experiments/E8/README.md -->. On the test sets all MedSAM conditions are 5-fold mean-probability ensembles <!-- src: experiments/test_stage/README.md -->.

**Post-hoc corrections and additions.** Three changes were made after results had been seen, each labelled as such in the result files. (i) *E2c loss correction.* The first E2c run used MedSAM's per-box Dice plus binary cross-entropy (BCE) on every box and collapsed on the training folds: the tiles learned to output empty masks (for example, only 5% of lesion-containing tiles gave any output with the final fold-1 checkpoint), while the whole-brain box over-segmented <!-- src: results/E2/tables/box_diagnostics_E2c_run1.csv -->. Using training-fold behaviour only, we changed the loss to BCE on every box plus Dice only on boxes containing lesion voxels. This loss was then used for E2b, E2c and E8; E2a's loss is mathematically identical under both settings and was not rerun. We also added a training-only collapse check that stops a fold if the lesion-box loss has not fallen to at most 0.8 of its starting value, or if fewer than 10% of lesion boxes give a non-empty prediction. The check runs from step 3,000 and every 1,000 steps after, both pooled and separately for the brain box and the tiles <!-- src: configs/experiment.yaml -->. The first run is kept as a documented failure. (ii) *Predicted-IoU filter (exploratory, decided after seeing E2c results).* For E2c and E8, boxes whose MedSAM predicted-IoU score falls below a threshold were set to empty masks. The threshold was chosen per outer fold by nested 4-fold CV within that fold's 12 training patients, maximising mean per-patient lesion F1, and the filter was not applied to any test set <!-- src: results/E2/cv_summary.md -->. (iii) *Metrics added after the freeze.* Segmentation-quality metrics (Section 2.3) and an additional E2a-versus-E1 comparison restricted to lesions detected by both models were computed on the frozen predictions without changing any earlier number <!-- src: results/test/summary.md -->.

**E7, MS-SAMLess (exploratory, after the freeze).** We adapted the public code of MS-SAMLess [REF] (upstream commit dde835c, used unmodified). Phase 1 trains a HarDNet-85 prompt learner with a frozen SAM ViT-B; phase 2 removes SAM and trains a light convolutional segmentor on the phase-1 backbone, which is the model evaluated <!-- src: experiments/E7/README.md -->. A HarDNet Segmentor baseline, the same network trained from ImageNet weights without phase 1 for the same total training (300 epochs), isolates the effect of SAM's guidance. We kept the published hyperparameters except for the following deviations: FLAIR/T1/T2 instead of FLAIR only; each training scan repeated 8 times per epoch to approximate the number of updates in the original setting; the last-epoch checkpoint instead of the best validation IoU; 1 mm resampling at inference as well as in training, mapped back to each case grid; and 5-fold ensembles on the test sets <!-- src: experiments/E7/README.md -->.

**E9, data scaling (exploratory, after the freeze; MSLesSeg labels follow a different annotation protocol).** One model per method was trained, without CV, on the combined set of 99 scans from 64 patients <!-- src: results/E9/training_set.json -->. nnU-Net used automatic planning on the combined data (1 mm isotropic, patch 128³) and its standard 1000 epochs <!-- src: experiments/E9/README.md -->. MedSAM E2c (82,500 steps) and the HarDNet Segmentor (29,700 iterations) kept the same number of updates per training scan as in their 12-scan CV folds <!-- src: experiments/E9/README.md -->. Each E9 model is compared with its frozen counterpart, which is a 5-fold ensemble.

### 2.3 Evaluation

Lesions were defined and matched with a port of animaSegPerfAnalyzer 4.2 [REF]. Lesions are 6-connected components larger than 3 mm³ in both masks <!-- src: configs/experiment.yaml -->. A reference lesion counts as detected if more than α = 0.10 of it is covered by the prediction, and if the predicted components that make up the first γ = 0.65 of that overlap each lie at most β = 0.70 outside the reference; predicted components are judged symmetrically for precision <!-- src: configs/experiment.yaml -->. We verified the port against the original binary on all 15 training patients for eight masks each (the out-of-fold nnU-Net prediction and the seven experts): all 120 comparisons of precision, recall, F1 and Dice agreed to within 5 × 10⁻⁷ <!-- src: results/E1/tables/anima_check_all15.csv -->.

Per scan we report voxel Dice, lesion-wise precision, recall and F1, and the number of false-positive (FP) lesions. Recall and FP lesions are also reported within three lesion-volume bins (< 20, 20–100 and ≥ 100 mm³) whose edges were fixed from the MSSEG training consensus (557 lesions: 133, 252 and 172) before any test result was seen <!-- src: configs/experiment.yaml -->. Lesion-count and lesion-volume agreement are reported as Bland–Altman bias, 95% limits of agreement and mean absolute error. Segmentation-quality metrics added after the freeze are: lesion-wise Dice, per-lesion 95th-percentile Hausdorff distance (HD95) and symmetric average surface distance, computed for each detected reference lesion against the union of predicted components that overlap it; whole-mask HD95; and whole-mask IoU <!-- src: results/test/summary.md -->. Lesion-wise metrics are conditional on detection, and whole-mask HD95 is dominated by distant false positives.

Means are over scans. 95% confidence intervals (CIs) are percentile bootstraps with 10,000 resamples of patients; pooled lesion recall uses a cluster bootstrap that resamples whole patients <!-- src: results/test/summary.md -->. Models are compared on the same scans with paired differences and a paired patient bootstrap CI, and with two-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank tests, Holm-adjusted within each comparison and test set across the per-scan metrics <!-- src: results/test/summary.md -->. Because ISBI's 21 scans come from 5 patients, ISBI bootstraps resample patients and Wilcoxon tests use one mean per patient (n = 5), so the smallest attainable two-sided p is 0.0625 <!-- src: results/test/summary.md -->. The MSSEG test scan msseg_test_c07_p08 has an empty consensus; it is reported separately by its FP lesions and excluded from all averages and tests <!-- src: results/test/summary.md -->.

### 2.4 Software and hardware

The analysis used Python 3.12 <!-- src: experiments/E1/README.md --> with PyTorch 2.11.0 (CUDA 13.0), nnU-Net v2 2.8.1, MONAI 1.6.1, segment-anything 1.0, NumPy 2.5.4, SciPy 1.18.1, nibabel 5.5.0 and SimpleITK 2.5.7 <!-- src: configs/requirements-lock-4090.txt -->, on a shared workstation with one NVIDIA RTX 4090 (24 GB), 24 CPU cores and 62 GB RAM <!-- src: experiments/E1/README.md -->. E1 training took about 46 GPU-hours in total <!-- src: experiments/E1/README.md -->. Each MedSAM fold took about 6 minutes including prediction <!-- src: experiments/E2/README.md -->. E7 took 4 h 23 min of training <!-- src: experiments/E7/README.md -->, and E9's nnU-Net took 7 h 57 min <!-- src: experiments/E9/README.md -->. Code, configuration and per-scan result tables are in the project repository [REF]; images and masks are not redistributed.

## 3 Results

### 3.1 nnU-Net in cross-validation and where it fails

In CV, E1 reached a mean Dice of 0.767 (95% CI 0.727–0.807) <!-- src: results/E2/tables/metrics.csv -->, lesion-wise precision 0.734 <!-- src: results/E2/tables/metrics.csv -->, recall 0.687 <!-- src: results/E2/tables/metrics.csv --> and F1 0.693 (0.627–0.757) <!-- src: results/E2/tables/metrics.csv -->, with 8.3 FP lesions per scan <!-- src: results/E2/tables/metrics.csv --> (Table 2). Its failures concentrate on small lesions (Figure 2). Pooled recall was 0.474 (63 of 133) for lesions < 20 mm³, 0.714 (180 of 252) for 20–100 mm³ and 0.866 (149 of 172) for ≥ 100 mm³ <!-- src: results/E2/tables/recall_by_size_bin.csv -->. Of the 165 missed lesions <!-- src: results/E1/cv_summary.md -->, 153 had no predicted voxel at all (67, 69 and 17 in the three bins) <!-- src: results/E1/cv_summary.md -->, so the misses are failures to find lesions, not near-misses of the matching rule. Small lesions also dominate the errors in the other direction: 83 of the 125 FP lesions (66%) were < 20 mm³ <!-- src: results/E1/cv_summary.md -->, and 31 FPs lay mostly inside reference lesions that the prediction left more than β = 0.70 uncovered, which the Anima rule counts as false positives <!-- src: results/E1/cv_summary.md -->. Performance varied by scanner: Philips patients had the highest small-lesion recall (51 of 86) but also the most FPs (13.0 per scan) <!-- src: results/E1/cv_summary.md -->.

### 3.2 MedSAM conditions in cross-validation: finding versus tracing

The MedSAM conditions separate the two parts of the task (Table 2, Figures 2 and 3). Given oracle boxes, E2a recovered 0.964 of 20–100 mm³ and 0.994 of ≥ 100 mm³ lesions <!-- src: results/E2/tables/recall_by_size_bin.csv --> and reached lesion F1 0.840 <!-- src: results/E2/tables/metrics.csv -->. Even so, it detected only 0.669 (89 of 133) of lesions < 20 mm³ <!-- src: results/E2/tables/recall_by_size_bin.csv -->, and 92% of its FP lesions were < 20 mm³ <!-- src: results/E2/tables/fp_lesions.csv -->. Without that information, E2c was far below E1: Dice −0.310 (−0.355 to −0.262), lesion F1 −0.484 (−0.561 to −0.408) and 104.8 more FP lesions per scan, all with Holm-adjusted p < 0.001 <!-- src: results/E2/tables/paired_comparisons.csv -->. E2c's pooled recall of lesions < 20 mm³ was 0.150 (20 of 133) <!-- src: results/E2/tables/recall_by_size_bin.csv -->, and 84% of its 1,697 FP lesions were < 20 mm³ <!-- src: results/E2/tables/fp_lesions.csv -->. The gap between E2a and E2c is therefore mainly a detection gap: the E2a − E2c difference in pooled recall of lesions < 20 mm³ was 0.519 (0.381–0.628) <!-- src: results/E2/tables/paired_comparisons.csv -->. The first E2c run, before the loss correction, was worse still (Dice 0.170, lesion F1 0.067) <!-- src: results/E2/tables/e2c_run1_metrics.csv -->; it would have led to the same E8 choice (difference 0.594) <!-- src: results/E2/cv_summary.md -->.

E2b, which places MedSAM boxes around E1's own detections, did not improve on E1. Dice changed by −0.084 (−0.096 to −0.070), with Holm-adjusted p < 0.001 <!-- src: results/E2/tables/paired_comparisons.csv -->, and FP lesions rose by 11.7 per scan (Holm-adjusted p = 0.013) <!-- src: results/E2/tables/paired_comparisons.csv -->, while lesion recall stayed close to E1's (−0.029, Holm-adjusted p = 0.53) <!-- src: results/E2/tables/paired_comparisons.csv -->. E8, the fix selected by the pre-registered rule, improved on E2c: lesion F1 +0.086 (0.049–0.126; Holm-adjusted p = 0.009) <!-- src: results/E2/tables/paired_comparisons.csv --> and roughly half the FP lesions (62.9 vs 113.1 per scan) <!-- src: results/E2/tables/metrics.csv -->. Its pooled recall of lesions < 20 mm³ barely changed (0.188 vs 0.150) <!-- src: results/E2/tables/recall_by_size_bin.csv -->, and E8 remained far below E1 (lesion F1 −0.398, Holm-adjusted p < 0.001) <!-- src: results/E2/tables/paired_comparisons.csv -->. The exploratory predicted-IoU filter had small effects: +0.016 lesion F1 for E2c and +0.002 for E8 <!-- src: results/E2/tables/iou_filter_E2c.csv --> <!-- src: results/E2/tables/iou_filter_E8.csv -->.

### 3.3 Test sets

On the MSSEG in-distribution test set (29 scans with lesions), E1 reached lesion F1 0.725 and Dice 0.744 with 5.1 FP lesions per scan <!-- src: results/test/tables/metrics.csv --> (Table 3, Figure 4). The ordering of the MedSAM conditions matched CV. E2c's lesion F1 was 0.235 <!-- src: results/test/tables/metrics.csv -->, a difference from E1 of −0.490 (−0.537 to −0.443) <!-- src: results/test/tables/paired_comparisons.csv -->. E8's was 0.376 (difference −0.349, −0.405 to −0.292) <!-- src: results/test/tables/paired_comparisons.csv -->, and E2b's 0.675 (difference −0.050, −0.070 to −0.030) <!-- src: results/test/tables/paired_comparisons.csv -->. All three differences have Holm-adjusted p < 0.001 <!-- src: results/test/tables/paired_comparisons.csv -->. E8 again improved on E2c, by +0.140 lesion F1 and 42.8 fewer FP lesions per scan (Holm-adjusted p < 0.001) <!-- src: results/test/tables/paired_comparisons.csv -->. E1 recalled 0.378 of lesions < 20 mm³; E2c and E8 recalled 0.116 and 0.171 <!-- src: results/test/tables/recall_by_size_bin.csv -->. On the empty-consensus scan, E1, E2a and E2b predicted nothing, whereas E2c produced 130 FP lesions and E8 produced 47 <!-- src: results/test/tables/empty_reference_msseg_id.csv -->.

On the unseen GE scanner (8 scans), E1's lesion F1 was 0.648, Dice 0.667 and 8.9 FP lesions per scan <!-- src: results/test/tables/metrics.csv -->. Recall of lesions < 20 mm³ was low for every model except E2a (E1 4 of 30, E2c 4 of 30, E8 3 of 30, E2a 20 of 30) <!-- src: results/test/tables/recall_by_size_bin.csv -->. The differences from E1 were large and in the same direction as in-distribution (E2c lesion F1 −0.493, E8 −0.393), but with 8 pairs no Holm-adjusted p fell below 0.05 (smallest 0.086) <!-- src: results/test/tables/paired_comparisons.csv -->. On ISBI, E1's lesion F1 was 0.649 against rater 1 and 0.616 against rater 2 <!-- src: results/test/tables/metrics.csv -->. E2c was lowest (0.079 and 0.076) and E8 intermediate (0.261 and 0.225) <!-- src: results/test/tables/metrics.csv -->. With 5 patients no ISBI comparison could reach p < 0.05 (smallest Holm-adjusted p 0.69), although the CIs of the lesion-F1 differences from E1 excluded zero for E2c, E8 and E2b against both raters <!-- src: results/test/tables/paired_comparisons.csv -->. For lesion count, E1's Bland–Altman bias on the in-distribution set was −10.0 lesions (95% limits −44.7 to 24.7); for volume it was −0.05 mL (−6.50 to 6.40) <!-- src: results/test/tables/agreement.csv -->.

### 3.4 Segmentation quality

Among detected lesions, E1 also outlined better than every MedSAM condition without reference boxes. On the in-distribution test set, lesion-wise Dice was 0.718 for E1 <!-- src: results/test/tables/segq_metrics.csv --> versus 0.480 for E2c, 0.541 for E8 and 0.621 for E2b <!-- src: results/test/tables/segq_metrics.csv -->. The differences from E1 were −0.238, −0.177 and −0.097, all with Holm-adjusted p < 0.001 <!-- src: results/test/tables/segq_paired_comparisons.csv -->. Per-lesion HD95 differed significantly only for E2c (+3.37 mm; Holm-adjusted p = 0.004) <!-- src: results/test/tables/segq_paired_comparisons.csv -->. Whole-mask HD95 mainly tracked false positives (16.8 mm for E1 vs 26.2 mm for E2c) <!-- src: results/test/tables/segq_metrics.csv -->.

Because E2a detects more of the harder lesions, we also compared E2a and E1 on the lesions both detected (Figure 5). In-distribution, E2a outlined these shared lesions less well in the 20–100 mm³ bin (−0.051, −0.081 to −0.020; Holm-adjusted p = 0.019) and similarly for ≥ 100 mm³ (+0.024, CI including zero) <!-- src: results/test/tables/segq_e2a_vs_e1_shared.csv -->. On the GE scanner the direction reversed: E2a was better on shared 20–100 mm³ lesions (+0.076, 0.057–0.095; Holm-adjusted p = 0.031) <!-- src: results/test/tables/segq_e2a_vs_e1_shared.csv -->. On ISBI, all bins were close to zero <!-- src: results/test/tables/segq_e2a_vs_e1_shared.csv -->. In CV the in-distribution pattern held in direction but not significantly (overall −0.037, −0.090 to 0.021) <!-- src: results/E2/tables/segq_e2a_vs_e1_shared.csv -->. Given correct boxes, MedSAM's outlines are therefore not uniformly worse than nnU-Net's: they were worse on in-distribution data and equal or better on the other scanners.

### 3.5 Exploratory: MS-SAMLess (E7) and data scaling (E9)

MS-SAMLess performed between the MedSAM conditions and E1. On the in-distribution test set its lesion F1 was 0.513, against 0.725 for E1 and 0.611 for its own HarDNet baseline <!-- src: results/E7/tables/metrics.csv -->. Its differences were −0.212 from E1 and −0.098 from the baseline, both with Holm-adjusted p < 0.001 <!-- src: results/E7/tables/paired_comparisons.csv -->, driven mainly by more FP lesions (24.6 vs 14.0 per scan) <!-- src: results/E7/tables/metrics.csv -->. SAM's guidance during training therefore did not help in our adaptation. Training on 99 scans instead of 12–15 (E9) left nnU-Net essentially unchanged in-distribution (lesion F1 0.725 → 0.726) <!-- src: results/E9/tables/overview.csv --> and left MedSAM E2c low (0.235 → 0.256) <!-- src: results/E9/tables/overview.csv -->. It made the HarDNet Segmentor worse (0.611 → 0.415; Holm-adjusted p < 0.001) <!-- src: results/E9/tables/overview.csv -->, which under-segmented MSSEG scans (median predicted-to-reference volume ratio 0.36) <!-- src: experiments/E9/README.md -->. These comparisons pit single E9 models against 5-fold ensembles and mix in a different annotation protocol, so they bound rather than measure the effect of more data.
